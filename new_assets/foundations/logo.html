The mark

A soft lowercase e drawn as one continuous stroke: the crossbar runs left out of the bowl, and the mouth at the lower right is real negative space rather than a shape laid over the top. It has no container, so it behaves like a letter beside the wordmark instead of an app tile — the people-software family, friendly and quiet, leaving the payroll story to the words. One path, one stroke weight, one colour: it survives a favicon, a single-ink print run and a photograph without redrawing.

Clear space and don'ts

Clear space on all four sides equals a quarter of the mark's height — 10 units in the 40-unit viewBox. Nothing enters that box, including the wordmark, which sits 6 units clear of the ink on the right.
Clear space = ¼ height
Never stretch
Never change the weight
No gradients or shadows
Also: never rotate or flip it, never close the mouth or square the caps, never put it inside a circle or rounded-square badge to fake an app tile (export logo.svg centred on a cobalt canvas instead), never set the wordmark in a face other than Geist 600, and never use the mark as a bullet, a spinner or a list icon.

Ownership

This mark was drawn for Emplora from scratch — a single-stroke lowercase e with a 13-unit bowl, a crossbar running past the bowl on the left, and a 315° terminal. It is not copied from any logo I know of, but nobody can promise a shape is globally unique, and a lowercase letterform is a crowded family: clearance is a legal question, not a design one. Before you file or print at scale, run a trademark search in your classes (Nice class 42 for the SaaS, class 9 for software) with an Indian trademark attorney, plus a reverse-image search on assets/logo.svg. If a conflict appears, the crossbar length and the terminal angle are the two dials that can move without losing the idea.
